Research & Insights

Evidence-based foundations for our solution

Empirical Foundations

Our project is grounded in educational data analytics and learning theory, justifying the multi-factor, early-alert approach.

Predictive Analytics Multi-Factor Modeling

Multi-Factor Predictive Modeling

Research by Siemens & Long (2011) and others highlights that single-factor approaches (e.g., GPA-only) consistently underperform compared to holistic assessments combining academic, behavioral, and demographic variables.

This insight informed the project's 40/30/20/10 weighted algorithm design:

  • 40% GPA: Academic performance as primary indicator
  • 30% Attendance: Behavioral engagement predictor
  • 20% Engagement: Social integration factors
  • 10% Financial: Economic stress indicators

Siemens, G., & Long, P. (2011). Penetrating the Fog: Analytics in Learning and Education. EDUCAUSE Review, 46(5), 30–39.

Early Intervention Retention

Timely Intervention Impact

Arnold and Pistilli's (2012) work on early alert systems demonstrated that predictive analytics can improve student retention by 12–21%, validating the need for the Pulse Tracker to facilitate rapid intervention.

Their Course Signals system at Purdue University showed that students who received early alerts were significantly more likely to succeed, with the greatest benefits for at-risk populations.

Arnold, K. E., & Pistilli, M. D. (2012). Course Signals at Purdue: Using Learning Analytics to Increase Student Success. In Proceedings of the 2nd International Conference on Learning Analytics and Knowledge (LAK '12, pp. 267–270). ACM.

Learning Theory Student Integration

Integration and Persistence Theory

Tinto's Student Integration Theory (1975) emphasizes the importance of both academic and social integration. This validates the inclusion and weighting of the Engagement Score (social/behavioral data) alongside GPA (academic data) as a key predictor.

Kuh et al. (2008) further demonstrated that student engagement metrics are strong predictors of persistence and achievement, supporting our multi-dimensional approach.

Tinto, V. (1975). Dropout from Higher Education: A Theoretical Synthesis of Recent Research. Review of Educational Research, 45(1), 89–125.

Kuh, G. D., Kinzie, J., Buckley, J. A., Bridges, B. K., & Hayek, J. C. (2008). College Students' Engagement in Learning: A Synthesis of Research. ASHE Higher Education Report, 33(2).

Financial Risk Dropout Prevention

Financial Stress as Predictor

Studies confirm that financial stress, particularly outstanding balances above specific thresholds, is a strong, non-linear predictor of dropout decisions (Paulsen & St. John, 2002), justifying the 10% Financial Component weight.

The research shows that even moderate financial stress can significantly impact academic performance and persistence, especially when combined with other risk factors.

Paulsen, M. B., & St. John, E. P. (2002). The Financial Nexus Between College Choice and Persistence. In J. C. Smart (Ed.), Higher Education: Handbook of Theory and Research, 17, 331-364.

Comparative Market Analysis

The Centralized Student Dashboard fills a critical market gap. Existing solutions are typically either expensive, high-complexity Enterprise Systems (e.g., Starfish) or inflexible, report-focused Data Warehouses.

Our solution is an intermediate approach: providing sophisticated predictive analytics and actionable interfaces (like the Pulse Tracker) without the high adoption barriers or cost of enterprise software. This makes it ideal for mid-sized institutions like HSU.
